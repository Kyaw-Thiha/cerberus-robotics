# RUN_001 — First validated Flat + Rough push-recovery pipeline (2026-09-03)

Session summary of the run that took Phase 0's locomotion training from "produces
a policy" to "produces a policy with a real, believable push-recovery number,"
after finding and fixing two real bugs in the push-disturbance mechanism. Written
for future-me: what happened, what the results actually mean, and what to do next.

## TL;DR

- Two real bugs found and fixed in the push-disturbance curriculum, not tuning
  issues: (1) the curriculum factor was updating ~24x too fast because
  `CurriculumManager.compute()` fires on every env reset, not once per training
  iteration; (2) "pushes" were actually continuous, unending forces (Isaac Lab's
  `permanent_wrench_composer` never clears between an interval EventTerm's
  triggers) instead of the short impulse the whole design assumed.
- With both fixed: **Flat push-recovery beats the reference** we anchored to
  (`isaaclab-go2-locomotion`, 87.5% at 120N) — we got **93% at 120N**, still 87%
  at 200N (well past the trained range).
- Rough trained clean (1500 iterations, no curriculum discontinuities) but its
  terrain-difficulty curriculum only reached level ~5.4 of ~9 by the end,
  short of the plan's "reaches highest difficulty level" criterion. **Update
  (2026-09-04, see "Post-session video/investigation findings" below): this is
  a genuine plateau, not a run cut short** — `terrain_levels` flattened from
  step ~1000 onward as reward/episode-length also plateaued, right as the push
  curriculum hit its own ceiling. More iterations at the same settings likely
  will not reach level 9; this needs a curriculum-pacing change, not just more
  training time. Recovery there is dominated by terrain difficulty (88-94%
  easy, 50-60% hardest), not push magnitude.
- Total pod cost for this whole session (bug hunt + both fixes + all training +
  all eval + all video + a wasted/redone Rough attempt): **6.73 hr, ~$4.98**
  (RTX 4090, $0.74/hr, RunPod Secure Cloud).
- **Post-session review (2026-09-04) found and fixed a real camera bug**: the
  showcase/play viewer was a fixed world-space camera, never tracking the
  robot — this is why push reactions were nearly invisible on video and why
  different-terrain-type Rough clips all visually looked like the same
  terrain. Fixed via `viewer.origin_type="asset_root"`; needs a pod-side
  smoke test to confirm. See details below.

## What went wrong first, and how it was found

The first real 1500-iteration Flat run (before any of this session's fixes)
finished with `Episode_Termination/base_contact` pinned near 100% for the whole
back half of training — virtually no episode surviving to its natural
time-out. The push-recovery eval sweep on that checkpoint (after separately
fixing a bug in the eval script itself — see below) showed a hard cliff: 96%
at 0N, 86% at 40N, **10% at 80N, 0% at 120N** — 0% at the *exact* peak trained
magnitude, against a reference number of 87.5% at that same magnitude on the
same robot. That mismatch (healthy-looking training curves, catastrophic eval
result at the trained magnitude) was the signal something structural was
wrong, not undertraining.

### Bug 1 — curriculum update cadence (~24x too fast)

`push_disturbance_curriculum` (a `CurriculumManager` term) was being invoked
from `ManagerBasedRLEnv._reset_idx()`, which fires on every environment reset
— with `num_envs=4096` running in parallel, that's roughly `NUM_STEPS_PER_ENV`
(24) times more often than "once per training iteration," the cadence
Hwangbo et al. 2019's `k0=0.3, kd=0.997` constants are actually calibrated
for. Confirmed empirically: `k_c` was reaching ~1.0 within the first ~8-10
iterations of *any* run, regardless of total budget. Fixed by throttling the
update to fire at most once per `env.common_step_counter // NUM_STEPS_PER_ENV`
change. See `core/curriculum.py` and `REFERENCES.md`'s "Curriculum update
cadence" section.

Two smaller related tuning changes made alongside this fix (both in
`core/push_disturbance_cfg.py`): raised `DENSE_INTERVAL_RANGE_S` from
`(3,5)` to `(5,7)` (pushing every 3-5s at full difficulty was structurally
squeezing out time to consolidate ordinary walking), and added
`INTERVAL_SATURATION_K_C=0.5` so the *interval* curriculum saturates at its
dense floor by the curriculum's halfway point instead of still changing
throughout the whole run (force/torque still ramp the full way to `k_c=1.0`).

This fix alone took Flat from **5% recovery at 0N** (79% fall rate with *zero*
push — i.e. broken baseline walking) to **96% at 0N**. It did not fix the
80-120N cliff.

### Bug 2 — sustained force, not a true impulse

Investigating the 120N cliff further: traced Isaac Lab's `Articulation`
source directly. `apply_external_force_torque` (used by both the training
curriculum and, until fixed, the eval's `apply_single_push`) sets force via
`asset.permanent_wrench_composer` — which, unlike the `instantaneous_wrench_
composer` (resets every physics step), **only resets at episode reset**.
So every "push" was actually a continuous, unending force: in eval, from
`push_time_s` to episode end (~4s, not a shove); in training, for the entire
interval between triggers (5-12s). We'd anchored `PEAK_FORCE_N=120.0` to
isaaclab-go2-locomotion's **Impulse** row (30-120N, but only 0.15-0.25s
duration) while actually implementing something that behaved like their
**Sustained load** row (continuous, multi-second) — whose own ceiling is just
10-40N. Wrong magnitude/duration pairing, not a tuning problem.

**Proof this was the real cause**: re-ran the eval sweep against the
*already-trained* (bug-2-unfixed) checkpoint, but through the *fixed* eval
harness alone — same weights, 0% → 75% at 120N, 10% → 87.5% at 80N. Same
policy, same weights, only the measurement changed. Confirmed the mechanism,
not the training, was the primary cause of the observed cliff.

Fixed via `core/push_impulse_event.py`: `apply_push_impulse` applies the
force as before but calls `schedule_push_clear`, paired with a second
`clear_expired_push_impulses` EventTerm at a degenerate `(step_dt, step_dt)`
interval that fires every `env.step()` and zeroes any push whose
`IMPULSE_DURATION_S=0.2` (Hwangbo/isaaclab-go2-locomotion's 0.15-0.25s
midpoint) has elapsed. Applied uniformly to training (`push_disturbance_cfg.
py`), the statistical eval (`push_recovery_eval.py`), and the eval's video
showcases (`push_recovery_showcase.py`, `push_recovery_showcase_flat.py` —
the latter is new this run, a Flat-only counterpart since the original
showcase script hard-requires a terrain-difficulty grid).

A full fresh (non-resumed) 1500-iteration Flat retrain under both fixes
produced the final numbers below.

### A smaller bug found in the same investigation

`push_recovery_eval.py`'s fall-detection was flagging every trial (including
clean recoveries) once its episode hit its own designed time-out, because
`dones` fires for both an actual fall and a benign time-out. Fixed by
splitting on `extras["time_outs"]` (only present because `is_finite_horizon`
defaults to `False`). This was the first bug found, before either of the two
above, and made the first eval sweep look far worse than reality until fixed.

### Flat vs. Rough: the gap, quantified, and what the evidence says about it

- **Reward**: Flat ends at 29.72, Rough at 17.30 — Rough **~42% lower**.
- **Fall rate during training**: Flat settles to ~3-4%, Rough stays at
  **~14-21%** throughout — roughly **4-5x higher**, never converging as low
  as Flat's.
- **Eval success rate**: Flat averages ~95% across all magnitudes. Rough at
  its easiest terrain (level 0, roughly Flat-comparable difficulty) averages
  **~88%** (7pp lower even on easy terrain); at hardest terrain (level 8) it's
  **~56.5%** (~40% relatively lower than Flat).

**Is this a hyperparameter problem?** No evidence for that. Diffed the actual
logged configs for both runs: PPO hyperparameters are **identical**
(`lr=0.001` adaptive, `clip_param=0.2`, `entropy_coef=0.01`, `gamma=0.99`,
`lambda=0.95`, `num_learning_epochs=5`, `num_mini_batches=4`,
`num_steps_per_env=24`, `num_envs=4096`, `seed=42`). The only architectural
difference is network size, and it runs the *opposite* direction of an
undertuning explanation: Rough uses a **larger** network (`[512,256,128]` vs
Flat's `[128,128,128]`) — Isaac Lab's own stock default for Rough (height-scan
observations need more capacity), not something misconfigured here. So Rough
isn't underpowered relative to Flat.

Two more points against "just needs more time": Rough's value loss is **~3x
Flat's and not decreasing** — flat around 0.027-0.033 since iteration ~600,
the same window `terrain_levels` also flattens (a genuinely-still-learning run
usually shows value loss still trending down). And `terrain_levels` moved only
+0.02 over the last 100 iterations (1400→1496) — at that rate, reaching level
9 from 5.4 would take several thousand more iterations at the *current*
pacing, not a small extension.

**Working hypothesis, not proven**: the terrain curriculum (Isaac Lab's stock
`terrain_levels_vel`) and this project's push-disturbance curriculum
(`curriculum.py`) are two *independent* curricula sharing the same iteration
clock, and they saturate in the same window — push `k_c` crosses 0.9 right
around iteration 800-900, exactly where `terrain_levels` flattens
(4.96→5.28→5.24). A robot needing to simultaneously learn harder terrain
*and* survive near-max random pushes is a harder joint problem than either
alone, and plausibly caps the achievable terrain level below what push-free
Rough training would reach. **This is a correlation, not a proven cause** — no
ablation has been run. The concrete, cheap next experiment: rerun Rough for a
few hundred iterations with `push_disturbance_curriculum` disabled (or its
ramp delayed well past terrain's typical convergence point). If
`terrain_levels` climbs meaningfully past ~5.4 without pushes in the way,
that confirms it — and the fix is to decouple the two curricula's schedules
(e.g. don't start ramping push force until terrain has had significant
iterations mostly to itself). If it still plateaus around 5, the cause is
something else (e.g. terrain promotion/demotion thresholds, reward shaping)
and needs separate investigation — but that's not worth guessing at before
running the cheap test. **Not supported by current evidence**: just extending
iteration count at current settings — both curves are flat for the last ~500
iterations, arguing against that alone being sufficient.

### Ablation: does push disturbance cap terrain-level progress? (2026-09-04) — ruled out

Ran the cheap test above for real: Rough, 1000 iterations, identical to the
baseline run in every way except `push_disturbance_curriculum` fully disabled
(force stayed at zero the entire run). Compared against baseline (with push)
at matched iterations:

| iter | terrain_lvl, w/ push (baseline) | terrain_lvl, no push (ablation) | reward, w/ push | reward, no push |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 300 | 1.01 | 1.05 | 13.61 | 16.40 |
| 600 | 3.88 | 4.17 | 15.08 | 18.44 |
| 800 | 4.96 | 5.18 | 15.79 | 17.85 |
| 900 | 5.16 | 5.42 | 15.42 | 19.97 |
| final | 5.40 (iter 1496) | **5.4491** (iter 999) | 17.30 | 19.29 |

**Terrain level plateaus at essentially the identical value (~5.4) whether
push is on or off.** Removing the disturbance entirely didn't let terrain
climb any higher, and the flattening happens the same way (visible leveling
off from iter ~900 onward) in both runs. **The compounding-curricula theory
above is ruled out** — push was never the bottleneck. The curriculum-gate
fix that theory implied (hold push at Hwangbo's K0 floor until terrain
crosses a threshold, then ramp) was implemented and then reverted once this
result came in; not deploying a fix for a disproven cause.

Reward is consistently and increasingly higher without push (gap widens from
~+2.8 at iter 300 to ~+4.5 by iter 900) — expected, since removing a real
disturbance removes its real reward cost; this is a side confirmation the
push mechanism is genuinely doing something (consistent with the physics/
statistics conclusion elsewhere in this doc), not evidence about terrain
capability specifically.

**Still open, not yet investigated**: *why* terrain plateaus at ~5.4. Two
untested candidates — the terrain curriculum's own promotion/demotion
thresholds naturally equilibrating around a difficulty where the policy
succeeds partially (by design for a level-based curriculum, not necessarily
a bug), or a genuine skill ceiling for this reward shaping/network/iteration
budget unrelated to push entirely. Neither confirmed; would need logging the
actual per-level promotion/demotion rates directly, a separate investigation
from this one.

### Rough training — a process note, not a result

The first Rough training attempt was killed by an under-estimated
`run_remote_job.sh --timeout 90m` at iteration 1300/1500 (Rough's true
budget needed ~100 min). Resuming from that checkpoint (`--resume --load_run
... --checkpoint model_1300.pt --max_iterations 200`) trained successfully to
1500 by rsl_rl's own iteration counter, **but the push curriculum's `k_c`
silently reset to `K0=0.3`** on resume, because that state lives in the
`ManagerTermBase` Python object, not in the saved checkpoint — so the last
200 "iterations" actually trained under a curriculum ramping from scratch
(reaching only `k_c≈0.52` by the end), not continuing the intended
progression toward `k_c≈0.987`. That resumed checkpoint was discarded
without evaluation — used a clean, single, uninterrupted 1500-iteration run
instead (150m timeout) for the numbers above. **Takeaway for future resumed
runs**: `--resume` is safe for the policy weights but not for any custom
curriculum term's in-memory state — either avoid resuming through a
curriculum-active window, or persist/restore that state explicitly if a
resume becomes unavoidable.

## Infra notes worth remembering

- `WANDB_API_KEY` and `R2_ACCOUNT_ID`/`R2_ACCESS_KEY_ID`/`R2_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY`/
  `R2_BUCKET` need to be in `/etc/cerberus_env.sh` (sourced by
  `run_remote_job.sh` at its own startup) to survive non-interactive SSH
  launches — exporting them in one interactive session does not persist to
  later `ssh host command` invocations. Both were missing at the start of
  this session's later launches (despite being in `/workspace/cerberus/.env`)
  and had to be appended to `/etc/cerberus_env.sh` mid-session. This should
  now be baked into `docker/start.sh` so it's automatic on future pods,
  instead of a manual fix-up every time.
- A periodic R2 sync failing (corrupted-transfer retry, or even an outright
  "Missing required env vars" failure) does not stop training — `run_remote_
  job.sh` logs a warning and retries next interval, or (on final sync
  failure) refuses to self-terminate and says so. Always worth a manual
  `sync_to_r2.py sync-run` pass at the end if periodic syncs were seen
  failing mid-run.
- `push_recovery_showcase.py` calls `apply_single_push` directly (not via an
  EventTerm), so the impulse-fix's new required `impulse_duration_s`
  parameter broke that call site silently until caught and fixed this
  session — any future signature change to `apply_single_push`/
  `apply_push_impulse` needs to be checked against all three callers
  (`push_recovery_eval.py`'s EventTerm params, `push_recovery_showcase.py`,
  `push_recovery_showcase_flat.py`), not just the EventTerm-registered one.

## Post-session video/investigation findings (2026-09-04)

After reviewing the showcase videos, five things looked suspicious enough to
investigate with dedicated (parallel) subagents, working from the local repo
and R2/wandb only — the RunPod pod from this run had already been terminated,
so nothing here was re-verified live in the sim.

1. **Is the push force actually reaching the robot?** No bug found. `asset_cfg`
   correctly resolves to just the base body, `force_range` is confirmed
   non-zero and ramping via the curriculum, and the physics is sane: 120-200N
   over the 0.2s impulse works out to a **1.6-2.7 m/s instantaneous velocity
   kick**, comparable to Rudin's own ±1 m/s standard. The 87-100% recovery
   rates in the eval sweep are direct empirical proof the force is real and
   effective. The "no visible reaction" impression is best explained by two
   compounding factors: a successful recovery from a 0.2s impulse is over in
   ~6 frames at 30fps, and by the camera bug below, which often has the robot
   poorly framed at exactly that moment.

2. **Rough terrain difficulty/sub-terrain type — is it actually varying?**
   `terrain_pinning.py`'s `pin_terrain` is correct: it writes
   `terrain.terrain_levels`/`terrain_types` from the intended per-env
   condition list, then recomputes `env_origins` with standard row=level,
   col=type indexing — verified against `TerrainImporter`'s own pattern, no
   off-by-one. `push_recovery_showcase.py`'s `condition_names` (used for
   clip filenames) and the lists passed to `pin_terrain` are built by
   iterating the exact same `conditions` list in the same order — filenames
   are guaranteed to match what was actually pinned.
   **The real cause was the camera bug (#3): every clip in a showcase run was
   recorded from the same fixed world-space point, regardless of which env
   was nominally selected**, so different-terrain-type clips necessarily all
   showed whatever terrain cell happens to sit under that one fixed point —
   consistent with the user's report that clips explicitly named for other
   sub-terrain types still visually looked like `boxes`. Terrain assignment
   itself was never broken.

3. **Camera framing — confirmed root cause.** `core/video_capture.py`'s
   `record_condition_clips()` calls `camera.set_view_env_index(env_idx)`
   once per clip. The viewer's `origin_type` was never set anywhere in this
   repo, so it defaulted to Isaac Lab's `"world"` mode — a camera fixed at a
   static world-space point, independent of which env is selected and never
   updated mid-clip. **Fixed** by setting `viewer.origin_type = "asset_root"`,
   `viewer.asset_name = "robot"` (plus a chase-cam `eye`/`lookat` offset) in
   both `UnitreeGo2FlatCerberusEnvCfg_PLAY` and
   `UnitreeGo2RoughCerberusEnvCfg_PLAY` (`core/go2_flat_env_cfg.py`,
   `core/go2_rough_env_cfg.py`) — applies to `play.py`'s walking video and
   both showcase scripts alike, since all three use the `-Play-v0` task
   variants. Isaac Lab recomputes eye/lookat from the tracked asset's root
   pose every step under `asset_root` mode, so this should fix both the
   static-framing and the mid-clip-drift problems in one change. **Not yet
   verified live** (no pod running this session) — needs a pod-side smoke
   test before trusting it fully; there's also a known upstream Isaac Lab
   issue (#551) about `asset_root` origin that's worth a quick sanity check
   against our pinned version.

4. **Were training-time (not just eval) videos taken?** No — confirmed by a
   full R2 listing of both run folders: only `videos/play/rl-video-step-0.mp4`
   exists per run (the one walking clip already reviewed). `train.py --video`
   was deliberately not passed this session (to avoid slowing the actual
   training run); the discussed "watch gait evolve across checkpoints" plan
   was never executed. Nothing is missing or lost — it was simply never
   generated.

5. **Rough undertraining** — see the corrected table and analysis in the
   "Rough — final" section above. Net: it's a plateau, not a truncated run.

## Terrain-curriculum mechanism check + sub-terrain diagnostic sweep (2026-09-04, second pod session)

Pulled Isaac Lab's actual pinned-version source (`terrain_levels_vel`,
`isaaclab_tasks/manager_based/locomotion/velocity/mdp/curriculums.py`, v2.3.2)
to check whether the terrain curriculum has a bug analogous to the push
curriculum's cadence bug. It doesn't: promotion/demotion is computed per-env,
right at that env's own reset, using that episode's own accumulated distance
— the correct design, unlike the push curriculum's original global-scalar bug.

The formula: promote if net displacement from episode start > `terrain
cell_size/2` (**fixed 4.0m**, cell size is 8m); demote if displacement <
`|commanded velocity| * episode_length_s * 0.5`. Commanded velocity is
`Uniform(-1,1)` on x and y, so its mean magnitude is ~0.77 m/s → mean demotion
threshold ≈ **7.65m**, actually *higher* than the promotion bar on average.
This corrects an earlier (wrong) guess in this doc that low commanded
velocity created an easy-to-stay-in "dead zone" below both thresholds —
the real numbers show promotion should be the *easier* bar under decent
tracking, not the harder one.

That reframes the plateau: since promotion mainly requires *surviving* long
enough in a 20s episode to rack up 4m of net progress, the more likely driver
is terrain-difficulty-driven falls before hitting that distance, not a
curriculum-math dead zone. The existing Rough eval sweep already showed this
gradient (0N recovery: 88% level 0 → 74% level 4 → 60% level 8) — but that's
pooled across all 6 sub-terrain types, so it couldn't say *which* terrain
type was actually driving the drop.

**A dedicated diagnostic pod session answered that.** Two things were run
against the existing baseline checkpoint (`model_1499.pt`, no retraining
needed):

1. **`play_terrain_showcase.py`**, full 6-type coverage at levels 0,4,5,6,7,8
   (31 clips) — this also verified the camera fix live for the first time: it
   works (clips correctly follow the robot), but **surfaced a real, separate,
   reproducible bug**: `ViewportCameraController._update_tracking_callback`
   throws `AttributeError: 'ManagerBasedRLEnv' object has no attribute
   'scene'` during `env.close()` teardown, every single time, because the
   `asset_root`-origin per-step tracking callback isn't unregistered before
   the scene is torn down. Happens *after* the clip is already written, so it
   doesn't corrupt data, but the script always exits uncleanly now — a
   follow-up fix, not yet done.
   Also hit a **genuine ~50-minute hang** on `level7_pyramid_stairs_inv` in
   the first full-batch attempt (509% CPU, GPU actively utilized, not a
   deadlock — a degenerate physics state, plausibly the robot wedged in the
   inverted-stairs geometry) that **ignored a `timeout 480` SIGTERM wrapper
   completely**. Killed manually; a retry of that exact same condition in
   isolation succeeded normally in under a minute, so this looks like a rare
   physics edge case, not a deterministic per-type failure. **Operational
   lesson**: use `timeout --signal=KILL` (or `--kill-after`) for any Isaac Sim
   invocation on a pod going forward — plain SIGTERM cannot be trusted to
   stop a stuck simulation process, confirmed directly here.
2. **`push_recovery_eval.py`** with the new `sub_terrain_type` CSV column
   (magnitudes 0,120 × terrain_levels 4-8 × 100 trials, 1000 envs, headless).
   Results, pooled across both magnitudes:

   | type | success rate (levels 4-8 pooled) |
   |---|---|
   | random_rough | 97.0% |
   | hf_pyramid_slope | 97.0% |
   | hf_pyramid_slope_inv | 91.0% |
   | boxes | 63.5% |
   | pyramid_stairs | 49.0% |
   | **pyramid_stairs_inv** | **12.0%** |

   Per-level breakdown (both magnitudes pooled): `pyramid_stairs_inv` is
   already at just 8% at level 4 (the *easiest* level tested) and stays in
   the 8-18% band all the way to level 8 — it's not a difficulty-scaling
   decline like the other types, it's uniformly bad from the start.
   `pyramid_stairs` declines 75%→38% from level 4 to 8 (real, terrain-level-
   driven decline). `boxes` declines 75%→48%. The slope types and
   `random_rough` stay strong (85-100%) across every level tested.

   This lines up exactly with `go2_rough_env_cfg.py`'s own config: `boxes`
   and `random_rough` are rescaled for Go2's smaller body size
   (`grid_height_range`, `noise_range`); `pyramid_stairs`/`pyramid_stairs_inv`
   and `hf_pyramid_slope*` are not. The slope types happen to still work fine
   unscaled; the stair types clearly don't — `pyramid_stairs_inv` most
   severely. **This is now a confirmed, data-backed finding, not a guess**:
   the terrain plateau is very plausibly dominated by these two unscaled
   stair sub-terrain types specifically, not a general policy skill ceiling
   and not a curriculum-math artifact.

## Rough retrain with stairs rescale — confirmed fix (2026-09-05, third pod session)

Implemented the rescale recommended above: `pyramid_stairs`/`pyramid_stairs_inv`'s
`step_height_range` cut 0.5x (stock `(0.05, 0.23)` -> `(0.025, 0.115)`), same factor
stock's own `boxes` rescale used. Ran a fresh 1500-iteration Rough training run
from scratch (not resumed) on this changed config, then re-ran the identical
per-sub-terrain-type push-recovery eval sweep against the new checkpoint for a
direct before/after comparison, then captured both video showcases.

**Training**: 1500 iterations, 5382.38s (~89.7 min). `Curriculum/terrain_levels`
reached **5.90** at the final iteration — clears the ~5.4 plateau every prior
Rough run (baseline and the no-push ablation) had flattened at. `terrain_levels`
was already at 5.39 by iteration 787 (52% through), essentially matching the old
run's *eventual* ceiling at the halfway point, then kept climbing through the
second half instead of flattening there — the qualitative signature of a real
unblock, not noise.

**Eval** (same sweep as the second pod session's diagnostic: magnitudes 0/120N x
terrain_levels 4-8 x 100 trials/cell, `sub_terrain_type` logged per trial):

| type | before (unscaled stairs) | after (rescaled stairs) |
|---|---|---|
| pyramid_stairs_inv | 12.0% | **71.0%** |
| pyramid_stairs | 49.0% | **84.5%** |
| hf_pyramid_slope_inv | 91.0% | 98.0% |
| random_rough | 97.0% | 95.5% |
| hf_pyramid_slope | 97.0% | 95.0% |
| boxes | 63.5% | 51.5% |

Both stair types improved by 35-59 points, confirming the rescale hypothesis
with data, not just plausible reasoning. `boxes` (untouched by this change)
dropped 12 points — most plausibly ordinary run-to-run training variance (this
is a fresh from-scratch run, not a resume, so a different random seed/rollout
history throughout), since nothing in this session touched its config, but
that's not independently proven and is worth watching in a future run rather
than treated as settled.

**Video capture**: `play_terrain_showcase.py` (15 clips, default levels/coverage)
